The problem with cheap crews

Why the $40 Mow-and-Go Is Costing You More Than You Think

Here's something the lawn-mowing apps won't tell you: the crew that shows up at your house on Wednesday mowed 10 other lawns that same day. If any of those lawns had large patch fungus, crabgrass, or gray leaf spot — and in Central Florida, at least two of them did — that disease arrived on your property on their blades.

The University of Florida's Institute of Food and Agricultural Sciences (UF/IFAS) documented this directly. Their guidance on large patch disease states that "the mower should be washed of all turf clippings before proceeding to the next site." For gray leaf spot, research confirms that "shoes and the lawn mower's wheels and blades are effective at spreading spores."

That's not our opinion. That's the state's own agricultural university.

Blue Daisy's standard

We sanitize our mowers and trimmers before every job — not after. It costs us 10 extra minutes per stop. We do it anyway because it's the professional standard, and because your lawn's health depends on it. This is not a bonus service. It's the minimum a real lawn-care company should do.
